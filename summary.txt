The day in brief: Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Ukraine, Russia and the US ended talks in Abu Dhabi agreeing in principle to swap 1,000 prisoners per side, but reached no ceasefire, and Kyiv rejected a US proposal covering unoccupied Donbas (NPR). Fighting continued: a Ukrainian strike damaged five fuel tanks and a hydrocracking unit at the Baltic port of Ust-Luga; Russian strikes hit six substations and a Chornomorsk market, killing three; NATO jets scrambled as drones strayed into Baltic airspace (Ukrainian General Staff; Just Security). Indonesia's Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka resigned, his aide said, giving his letter to President Prabowo after courts voided his education credentials and 120 lawmakers moved to impeach him; the palace had not confirmed acceptance (Reuters). Iran's negotiator Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz, shut 250 days, would never regain its pre-war status (Al Jazeera); Marco Rubio said economic pressure made combat unnecessary (The New York Times); Brent settled 1.4% higher at $92.76. At COP31, UN chief Antonio Guterres warned slow climate action put the world on a path to destruction; the WMO put 2026 among the four warmest years on record (Reuters). Israeli strikes killed seven in Gaza, among them a father and his 13-year-old son (Gaza health officials); Israel also hit southern Lebanon; Saudi Arabia intercepted seven Houthi missiles and drones, it said. The US charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 plots in the US and Europe, and Treasury Secretary Bessent moved onto a military base amid Iranian threats (Just Security). House Republicans blocked a move to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's Senate seat (AP). Trump said he would meet Xi Jinping at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18-19 November. In Sudan, the army advanced in West Kordofan amid drone strikes on the power grid. British police arrested a US Army officer with a NATO unit on suspicion of spying for Russia.
