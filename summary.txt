The day in brief: Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Ukraine, Russia and the US ended talks in Abu Dhabi agreeing in principle to swap 1,000 prisoners per side, but reached no ceasefire, and Kyiv rejected a US proposal covering unoccupied Donbas (NPR). Fighting continued: Ukraine damaged five fuel tanks and a hydrocracking unit at Ust-Luga port; Russian strikes hit six substations and a Chornomorsk market, killing three; NATO jets scrambled over drones in Baltic airspace (Ukrainian General Staff; Just Security). Iran's Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz, shut 250 days, would never regain its pre-war status (Al Jazeera); Brent rose 1.4% to $92.76, and Rubio said economic pressure made war unnecessary (NYT). The US charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 plots across the US and Europe; Treasury Secretary Bessent moved to a military base amid Iranian threats (Just Security). Indonesian Vice President Gibran resigned, his aide said, handing President Prabowo his letter after courts voided his credentials and 120 lawmakers moved to impeach; the palace had not confirmed acceptance (Reuters). Spain's first general strike in 14 years: unions said four million joined the housing-cost walkout; over 300 flights were cancelled, dozens arrested, 18 days before a snap election (Reuters; AP). Storm Charles cancelled over 200 flights in Portugal and closed schools as it made landfall (Reuters). At COP31, UN chief Guterres warned slow climate action put the world on a path to destruction, as the WMO ranked 2026 among the four warmest years on record (Reuters). Israeli strikes killed seven in Gaza, among them a father and his 13-year-old son (Gaza health officials); Israel also hit southern Lebanon, and Saudi Arabia intercepted seven Houthi missiles and drones, it said. House Republicans blocked a move to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's Senate seat (AP). Sudan's army advanced in West Kordofan amid drone strikes on the grid.
