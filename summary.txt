Wednesday 11 November 2026. Ukraine, Russia and the United States ended two days of talks in Abu Dhabi agreeing in principle to swap about 1,000 prisoners of war per side, but no ceasefire, and Kyiv rejected a US proposal covering unoccupied parts of Donbas (NPR). The fighting continued: Ukraine said its strike on Russia's Baltic oil port of Ust-Luga damaged five fuel tanks and a hydrocracking unit, Russian strikes hit six power substations and a market in Chornomorsk, killing three, and NATO jets scrambled after drones strayed into Baltic airspace (Ukrainian General Staff; Just Security). At the COP31 leaders' summit in Antalya, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told heads of government the slow climate response had put the world on a path to destruction; the WMO put 2026 on course to be among the four warmest years recorded, Turkiye promoted electricity reaching 35% of energy demand by 2035, and the United States stayed away for a second straight year (Reuters). Iran's negotiator Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz, closed for more than 250 days, would never return to its pre-war status (Al Jazeera), while Secretary of State Marco Rubio said economic pressure made major combat operations unnecessary (The New York Times); Brent crude settled 1.4% higher at $92.76 a barrel. Israeli strikes killed seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son (Gaza health officials), and struck targets across southern Lebanon; Saudi Arabia said it intercepted seven Houthi missiles and drones. House Republicans blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's Senate seat (AP). President Trump said he expects to meet China's Xi Jinping at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18-19 November. British authorities arrested a US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia. The Nasdaq climbed 1.6% as AI shares rallied. In Sudan, the army advanced in West Kordofan as drone strikes hit the power grid.
